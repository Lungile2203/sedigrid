Dust monitoring for solar panels in the Free State

SediGrid compares each array with a clean reference panel, tells you whether a drop in output is dust, cloud or a fault, and books a trained local crew only when a clean will pay for itself.

We are a student team at the Central University of Technology. The sensor and dashboard are working prototypes, and the live demo below runs the whole plan from alert to verified clean. The pilot that measures real Free State sites starts in June 2027.

The problem

Dust from farms, mine dumps and gravel roads settles on panels through the dry season. Bloemfontein gets 9 to 14 mm of rain a month from June to August, and a Stellenbosch study found panels need about 6 mm every four to six weeks to stay clean. Light rain and dew can make it worse by gluing the dust to the glass.

The inverter app shows that output dropped, not why. Without a measurement, an owner can't tell dust from cloud, shade or a wiring fault, so a cleaning decision is a guess. Some owners clean on a calendar and pay for cleans they didn't need. Some use hard water or pressure washers that can scratch the glass and void the warranty. Some wait and lose output for months.

Checking and cleaning panels is entry-level work that can be taught. Free State unemployment is 37.3% (Stats SA, Q2 2026), and nothing yet links owners who need the work done to young people who could be trained for it.

Where it applies

Each site gets one sensor node beside the array: a clean reference panel, an ESP32 board and a rain sensor. The cleaning method depends on the dust.

How it works

A job only counts as done when the sensor reading recovers.

  1. Measure. The sensor node compares the array with the clean reference panel every few seconds.
  2. Decide. The dashboard rules out cloud, faults and rain, shows the loss in rand, and says clean now or wait.
  3. Prescribe. It picks the cleaning method for the dust and panel type, within the panel maker's warranty rules.
  4. Clean. A trained local crew gets the job, the method and a safety checklist.
  5. Verify. The ratio has to recover. The owner gets before and after readings; the crew member gets a verified job on their record.

The sensor node

Two small panels side by side, each with its own 50 Ω load. Panel A is kept clean; Panel B sits on the array and collects dust. The node reads both every 2 seconds, and the buzzer sounds when soiling stays at 8% or more for three readings.

Working with installers and O&M companies

Large plants already have maintenance contracts, and an installer we spoke to in Parys includes cleaning in hers. We would give companies like that local dust data, a clean-or-wait call per site and trained crews when they need them, and serve the farms, mine plants, business roofs and homes that fixed schedules don't reach.

What we don't know yet

  • Nobody has measured soiling losses in the Free State. We don't quote a figure; the pilot will measure one.
  • Losses vary widely. A Northern Cape plant lost 1 to 2% with regular rain; studies near heavy dust sources found over 20%.
  • The mine and smelter studies are indirect: the tailings studies measured dust, not panels.
  • We found no survey of how South African owners actually clean their panels.
  • Our prototype uses two mini panels. Real sites will also need inverter data.

The pilot

We want to measure soiling weekly through one dry season at three sites, train the first local crew, and publish the results. We are looking for funders, site owners, installers and training partners.

What the funding pays for

  • Three sensor nodes: reference panel, ESP32, rain and temperature sensors, weatherproof enclosure and mobile data.
  • Training for the first youth crew: cleaning methods by dust type, electrical isolation and working safely on roofs and arrays.
  • Weekly measurement from June to September at CUT's 153.7 kWp campus plant, a goldfields mine plant and a western Free State farm.
  • A public report with the soiling rate, rain recovery and cost per verified clean at each site.

What partners get

  • Funders: named in the report, with progress updates and access to the dashboard during the pilot.
  • Site owners: a node on your array for the season and a clean-or-wait call with every reading.
  • Installers and O&M companies: local dust data for quotes, and first access to trained crews.
  • Training partners: a verified job record for every learner who cleans with us.
